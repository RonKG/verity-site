## 2026-09-26 — Solving the cold-start: getting owners to come to us

### Where things stand

The project has stalled on supply, not software. The site is built, but the listings and
metrics currently on it are a **vision mock** — they represent what a semi-mature product
offering would look like, not real traction. There are no real listings yet.

The core constraint: I'm not in Kenya, so I can't door-knock owners, attend viewings, or
build the face-to-face relationships this market runs on.

The core bet: Kenyan properties worth hundreds of millions of shillings are routinely
marketed with poor imagery. Genuinely professional photography is a credible wedge that
attracts the right attention and justifies a premium, curated position.

The question this entry answers: given those constraints, how do I get property owners to
seek me out instead of me chasing them?

### Two reframes that reorder everything

**1. I'm standing on the demand side, not the supply side.** The buyers who most need
trustworthy remote verification and images good enough to buy sight-unseen are diaspora
Kenyans — and I am one, living among them. In Kenya, the thing that makes an owner or agent
return a call is not a website. It's "I have a buyer." Most of the plays below are
downstream of building that buyer list.

**2. Sell exclusive imagery, not exclusive listings.** Sole mandates are rare in Kenya and
owners actively distrust them — the instinct is that exclusivity limits reach. A Runda house
is routinely listed by a dozen brokers at a dozen prices. But if I own the only good images
of a property, I have de facto exclusivity of presentation even when ten brokers list it.
Same moat, without fighting the culture.

### The plays, ranked by realism from abroad

**1. Architects and interior designers as the trojan horse.**
They have working relationships with exactly the owners I want, they badly want
professional photography of completed projects, and most can't justify paying for it.
Reachable entirely from abroad via Instagram, LinkedIn, and the Kenya Institute of
Architects. Offer a free, properly-produced shoot of a completed project in exchange for
access and credit. I get a library of stunning Kenyan homes; they get portfolio assets; the
owner is flattered rather than sold to. When that owner sells, I'm the obvious call — and
their neighbours have seen the feature.

- Start by: listing 20 Nairobi architects and designers doing prime residential work,
  picking the 5 whose work is best-photographed-badly, and sending a specific note about one
  named project.
- Cost: one shoot, roughly KSh 40–120k ($300–900).
- Time to first listing: 4–8 months. Relationship play, not a transaction.
- Fails when: I treat it as lead-gen and they smell it.
- Double down when: an architect introduces me to a second owner unprompted.

**2. Coastal villas as the beachhead — not Nairobi.**
Diani, Watamu, Vipingo, and Kilifi are the structural match. Owners are often foreign or
diaspora, commercially minded, already marketing publicly on Airbnb and Booking.com (so
they're reachable from abroad and have no discretion problem), acutely aware that bad photos
cost them bookings, and a meaningful share are quietly open to selling. Karen and Muthaiga's
top tier is the opposite: politically exposed, family-trust-owned, structurally allergic to
public listing. Starting in Nairobi means fighting for the properties least willing to be
shown.

- Start by: shortlisting 15 high-end coastal villas from short-let platforms, checking which
  have weak imagery, and offering a free reshoot they can use for bookings with listing
  rights as the trade.
- Cost: one trip's shooting, or hire local — coastal drone and villa shooters exist.
- Time to first listing: 2–4 months.
- Fails when: I end up with rental-marketing clients instead of sale mandates.
- Double down when: an owner says "actually, would you sell it?"

**3. Build the diaspora buyer list, then sell access to it.**
A monthly written brief for diaspora considering a Kenyan purchase: honest area analysis,
title-fraud patterns, what KSh 30M actually buys in Karen versus Diani, what went wrong for
people who bought off-plan. All buildable from public Hass Property Index and KNBS data,
from my desk. Distribute through Kenyan diaspora networks in Dallas, Atlanta, Boston,
Minneapolis, London — church networks and diaspora SACCOs and chamas carry far more weight
than ads. At 500–2,000 engaged subscribers the pitch inverts: I stop asking for listings and
start telling developers I have buyers.

- Start by: writing three issues before promoting anything, then approaching group admins as
  a person with something useful, not a brand.
- Cost: time plus ~$30/month.
- Time to first listing: 6+ months, but it's the asset that makes plays 4 and 6 work.
- Fails when: I post like a marketer into groups already saturated with spam.
- Double down when: people reply asking me to find them something.

**4. Trade flagship media to developers for launch exclusivity.**
Developers are the one supply source that is institutional, email-reachable from abroad,
budgeted, sales-hungry, and specifically diaspora-hungry. Offer to produce a film and photo
set of a show unit at my cost, in exchange for exclusive online first-look and distribution
to my list. Fastest route to real inventory at real volume.

- Start by: targeting mid-size prime developers rather than the majors, whose marketing is
  agency-controlled.
- Cost: one production, KSh 80–200k ($600–1,500).
- Time to first listing: 2–5 months.
- Fails when: I attach a trust brand to a developer who doesn't deliver. The Cytonn collapse
  is the exact reputational risk, and off-plan diaspora sales are where it bites hardest.
  Vet completion track record before shooting anything.
- Double down when: a developer asks about the next project.

**5. Publish a quarterly prime index and let the press make me findable.**
Kenyan business media quotes Hass Consult constantly and has been short of credible
independent research since Cytonn's troubles. A defensible quarterly read on prime Nairobi
and coastal pricing, built from public data, can earn citations in Business Daily and The
Standard. Owners and agents don't search for listing platforms, but they read the property
pages — and a cited index makes me a known quantity before I ask for anything.

- Start by: publishing one issue with a fully transparent methodology.
- Cost: time only.
- Fails when: the methodology is soft and a real analyst picks it apart.
- Double down when: I'm quoted once.

**6. Recruit the photographers before I need them.**
The partners page already exists and this costs nothing. Kenya has genuinely excellent
architectural photographers and drone operators, and the arbitrage is the core economic fact
of this business: a day of top Nairobi architectural work costs less than a US
photographer's hourly rate. Each one arrives with their own relationships to agents,
architects, and owners. Drone work is regulated by KCAA and requires a licensed operator
with registered equipment — insisting on that is both compliance and a credibility signal.

- Start by: DMing ten on Instagram with real work offered, not a "partner network" pitch.

### The cold start, directly

Pay for one shoot myself. In dollar terms it's small, and it's the only artifact that makes
the argument — I'm claiming photography is the wedge, so the wedge has to exist before
anyone believes it. Do it on a coastal villa, where access is easiest, and publish it as a
single, deliberately lonely feature: **Issue 01**. One property presented immaculately is
far more persuasive than a grid of placeholders, and it's honest about where I am.

### Live risks in the current build

- **The demo data.** I know it's a vision mock; a Nairobi owner or a journalist does not.
  "2,400 verified properties, KSh 82B" with nothing behind it reads as fabrication in a
  market where fraud anxiety is the defining emotion. Either password-gate the mock and ship
  a one-page holding site plus the brief, or relabel it explicitly as a product preview.
- **"72-hour verification."** A promise with real liability. Performing title verification
  for reward edges toward territory governed by the Estate Agents Act (Cap 533) and the
  advocates' monopoly on legal work — worth an hour with a Kenyan advocate, and better
  restructured as "we commission an independent advocate's title search," letting a partner
  firm own that risk.
- **The Verified / Premium / Elite badge ladder.** Reads as pay-to-play, which quietly
  contradicts the trust claim it's meant to support.

### Ruled out

- Cold-emailing owners. There's no ownership-to-email path in Kenya the way US public
  records allow.
- Paid ads aimed at owners. Owners never search for listing platforms.
- Competing with Property24 and BuyRentKenya on SEO with no inventory.
- Diaspora property expos as a first move, before there's anything to show.
- A "list your property" form — most dangerous because it looks like success. The first
  inbound will be brokers with recycled junk stock, not owners, and accepting them destroys
  the curation premium that is the entire business.

### Working assumptions behind the above

Based in the US, ~10 hrs/week available, a few hundred dollars a month with one-off capacity
around $1–2k, no staff in Kenya but some family and social network there, 6-month horizon to
a first real listing. If any of these change materially, the ranking changes.
